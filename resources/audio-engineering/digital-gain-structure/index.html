Digital Gain Structure

A practical guide to setting clean levels on digital consoles for live audio.

Keep Your Signal Flow Clean

Digital gain structure starts with the first gain stage and continues through the channel, bus, and master outputs. When each stage is set correctly, the mix preserves clarity, headroom, and dynamic range.

  • Target an operating level around -18 dBFS on channel meters for normal program material.
  • Allow peaks to land near -6 dBFS on the master bus to preserve headroom and avoid clipping.
  • Use trim and preamp gain to get a healthy signal before the fader, then balance with the fader rather than pushing input gain too high.

On digital consoles, many processing blocks are optimized for floating-point headroom. Keep trims and bus levels in a stable range so compression, EQ, and effects have room to operate without distortion.

Practical steps

  1. Set the input gain so a strong vocal or instrument sits near -18 dBFS on the channel meter.
  2. Check the channel bus and master bus meters — if the master is too hot, lower the bus sends or channel faders.
  3. Confirm that the output path has consistent gain structure from stage to stage, especially when using subgroups or matrix buses.

Good gain structure reduces noise, protects against clipping, and makes the console easier to mix from the first cue through the final output.

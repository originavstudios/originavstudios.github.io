The Exposure Triangle

How aperture, shutter speed, and ISO work together to shape exposure and creative image control.

Balancing Light and Motion

The exposure triangle is the core concept for any photographer. It describes the three settings that determine exposure and creative effect:

  • Aperture controls how much light enters the lens and affects depth of field.
  • Shutter speed controls how long the sensor is exposed and how motion is captured.
  • ISO controls sensor sensitivity, which affects brightness and image noise.

Start by selecting the aperture you need for the look you want, then adjust shutter speed to handle motion, and finally raise or lower ISO to achieve the right exposure without introducing too much noise.

Creative tradeoffs

  • Wide aperture gives a shallow focus look, while narrow aperture keeps more of the scene sharp.
  • Fast shutter speed freezes action, while slower shutter speed adds motion blur.
  • Low ISO keeps images clean, while higher ISO helps in low-light situations at the cost of more grain.

Once you understand the exposure triangle, you can make intentional choices for both technical accuracy and artistic expression.
